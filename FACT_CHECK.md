# Fact-check notes

Last audited: **August 7, 2026**

This timeline is a selective teaching map, not a complete intellectual genealogy. A displayed year identifies the cited publication, English-language edition, institutional launch, or documented event. It does not automatically mean that the underlying idea began in that year.

The current dataset contains **90 cited milestones**: 66 in the learning-theory timeline and 24 in the AIED lineage.

## Evidence labels

- **Scholarly source**: DOI, publisher record, or an archived copy of an original scholarly work.
- **Official or institutional source**: a society, foundation, government agency, university, or conference record.
- **Bibliographic record**: a library or book-catalog record used to verify publication metadata.
- **Preprint**: a public manuscript that has not been treated here as peer-reviewed or established theory.

The labels describe the linked source, not the overall importance or truth of a theory.

## Date qualifications shown in the app

- Piaget's 1952 milestone is identified as the cited English-language edition.
- Vygotsky's 1978 milestone is identified as a posthumous edited English collection.
- Publication, conference, organizational, and preprint dates are distinguished in the detail panel and popup.

## Remaining limits

- The map is intentionally selective and compresses debates within each tradition.
- A citation verifies the specific milestone and date basis; it does not establish a single uncontested origin for an entire school of thought.
- EGRA's deeper historical provenance remains unresolved in the sources reviewed, so the app makes no founder or origin-year claim.
- Agentivism is current and provisional. It should not be presented as a consensus learning theory.

Corrections with a stronger primary, publisher, library, or official source are welcome through the repository's issue tracker.
